## How to use

Open `tools/truss-studio.html` in a browser (requires its adjacent `truss-fem.js` and `truss-fem-ui.js`). Select a reference model or edit **Joints**: unique ID, X/Y in metres, Fix X/ Fix Y, forces Fx/Fy in kN (positive right/up). In **Members**, enter start/end IDs, E in GPa, area in mm² and optional density in kg/m³. The **Section property helper** in the material breakdown calculates area, centroidal Ixx/Iyy, elastic section moduli Sx/Sy, extreme-fibre distances cX/cY and radii of gyration rx/ry for solid/hollow rectangles and circles, plus open channels, I-sections and L-angles. L-angles also report Ixy, principal I1/I2 and the major-principal-axis angle; the selected centroidal/principal moment is applied to one member. Sx = Ixx/cX and Sy = Iyy/cY; rx = sqrt(Ixx/A) and ry = sqrt(Iyy/A). Rectangle Ixx is about the horizontal centroidal axis parallel to width; circle I is about any centroidal diameter. These are geometric properties, not design resistances. Optional Euler estimate inputs are I (second moment of area, mm⁴) and K (dimensionless effective-length factor); provide both to calculate Pcr. Either property may be stored alone without producing an Euler estimate; the geometry/member preview remains available and the status explains when the pair is incomplete. Click Analyze truss. Results show member tension/compression force (kN), stress (MPa), joint displacements (mm), support reactions (kN), residual/energy verification and, when I/K are supplied, a compression-only ideal Euler comparison. Gray dotted members represent the original truss; colored amplified lines represent deformed geometry (green tension, orange compression). Change scale or label visibility, export the model as JSON, results as CSV, diagram as SVG; imported legacy SI JSON is converted explicitly.

## Numerical contract

The FEM solver models 2D pin-connected, axial-only, linear-elastic, small-displacement trusses with static loads. Each bar stiffness is `EA/L * [-c,-s,c,s]^T [-c,-s,c,s]`; global K is assembled, fixed zero-displacement DOFs eliminated, free system diagonal-scaled and solved with pivoting. Member axial extension is projected relative endpoint displacement; positive axial stress is tension. Reactions are `K*u-F`. Force balance, free-DOF residual, and twice strain energy = external work are checked. The solver itself does not model geometric buckling, post-buckling, yielding, thermal loads or connection behavior.

## Section-property helper

The standalone helper uses dimensions in mm and returns area in mm², centroidal second moments in mm⁴, elastic section moduli in mm³, extreme-fibre distances in mm and radii of gyration in mm:
- Solid rectangle: A = b·h, Ixx = b·h³/12, Iyy = h·b³/12.
- Concentric hollow rectangle: subtract the centered inner rectangle from the outer rectangle for A, Ixx and Iyy.
- Solid circle: A = π·d²/4, Ixx = Iyy = π·d⁴/64.
- Concentric hollow circle: subtract the inner circle from the outer circle for A and equal centroidal I.
- Open channel: the web is on the left and both flanges extend right. It is assembled from a full-height web and two non-overlapping flange rectangles.
- I-section: two full-width flanges and a centered non-overlapping web rectangle.
- L-angle: one full-height left leg and one bottom leg extending right, decomposed into two non-overlapping rectangles so the shared corner is counted once.

For channel and I-section, the helper computes the composite area centroid and both centroidal moments with the parallel-axis theorem. Both idealized open profiles are symmetric about the horizontal centroidal axis, so the horizontal/vertical centroidal axes are principal for these geometries. The channel centroid is reported from the outside web face and bottom edge; the I-section centroid is centered for symmetric inputs. Select Ixx (horizontal centroidal axis) or Iyy (vertical centroidal axis) before applying the selected value to the member. For bending about X, cX is the larger distance from the centroid to the top/bottom extreme fibres; for bending about Y, cY is the larger distance to the left/right extreme fibres. The channel's offset centroid therefore uses the farther outside edge when calculating cY.

For the L-angle, Ixy is the centroidal product of inertia Σ[Aᵢ(xᵢ−x̄)(yᵢ−ȳ)] because each aligned rectangle has zero local product of inertia. The principal moments are I1/I2 = (Ixx+Iyy)/2 ± sqrt(((Ixx−Iyy)/2)² + Ixy²); the I1-axis angle is 0.5·atan2(−2Ixy, Ixx−Iyy), counter-clockwise from +x. The reported angle uses Ixy = ∫(x−x̄)(y−ȳ)dA and the I1 major-moment convention. When a solid rectangle or solid circle is selected, the helper renders a live, accessible SVG with its outer dimensions, centroid and centroidal axes; the circle sketch shows D and two perpendicular diameter axes. When an L-angle is selected, it renders B/H/t dimensions, centroid and the major principal axis. All three previews update as dimensions are edited, clear stale outlines when dimensions are invalid, and keep the SVG accessible name synchronized with the visible caption. These are geometry previews, not stress contours or FEM results.

The section-property helper returns an immutable centroid for solid and concentric hollow rectangles, solid and concentric hollow circles, and the open channel, I-section and L-angle. Rectangle centroid coordinates are measured from the lower-left outer corner; for a concentric void they remain at (B/2,H/2). Circular centroid coordinates are measured from the lower-left outer corner of the diameter-sized bounding square and remain at (D/2,D/2) for a concentric void. The live rectangle and solid-circle sketches consume this canonical result rather than recomputing the centroid in the UI. The helper also reports Sx = Ixx/cX and Sy = Iyy/cY, plus rx = sqrt(Ixx/A) and ry = sqrt(Iyy/A). These are elastic geometric section properties about the stated centroidal axes; they are not code resistance, allowable stress or a substitute for a complete member stability/design check. The section moduli and radii are displayed for reference and are not used by the axial-only FEM or the optional Euler estimate.

The open-section geometry assumes sharp corners, uniform web/flange thickness, no lips, no fillets and no eccentricity. The L-angle assumes two equal leg thicknesses meeting at a sharp right angle, with no fillet or toe radius. It does not calculate torsional constant, shear centre, shear area, warping, connection effects or code resistance. The formulas describe ideal prismatic cross-sections only.

All dimensions must be finite and strictly positive, with web thickness smaller than B and two flange thicknesses smaller than H. Area, moments, section moduli and radii that underflow or overflow are rejected before any member is changed. Applying a valid result updates only the selected member's area and I; E, density, K and other members are preserved.

## Optional ideal Euler member estimate

The non-solving material preview can report the ideal elastic critical load `Pcr = π² E I / (K L)²` when both second moment of area `I` and effective-length factor `K` are supplied. Current Studio JSON/UI uses `I` in mm⁴; legacy SI JSON uses `I` in m⁴ and converts it on import. `K` is dimensionless. The reported `Pcr` is in kN. Missing I/K leaves the estimate blank; supplying only one, non-positive/non-finite values, or an unrepresentable result is rejected. After solving, only compressive axial force is compared with `Pcr`; the result reports utilization `Pcomp/Pcr` and load ratio `Pcr/Pcomp`. Tension is not applicable and zero force is reported as no compression demand without division. This remains an ideal elastic screen, not a geometric buckling solver or code-compliant resistance check; it does not include imperfections, residual stress, local/flexural-torsional modes, inelasticity, code resistance factors or post-buckling. It is an educational idealization, not a certified design check; consult a qualified structural engineer.

## Compatibility

Previous TrussStudio API still exposes `TrussStudio.solve(model)` and `TrussStudio.example` with SI N/Pa/m² units and legacy output shape. The UI now accepts kN/GPa/mm². JSON import recognizes legacy member field `A` (m²) and converts load forces in N, E in Pa, and A in m² to the new form; exported JSON uses explicit new-form keys and units. Existing `index.html` distribution and source/bundle hashes are unchanged, but the companion page requires its relative JS assets, including for offline use.
